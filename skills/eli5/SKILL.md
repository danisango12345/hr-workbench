Explain a website, text snippet, screenshot, or other piece of content the user shares, in plain, easy-to-digest language.

## 1. Identify what's being explained

Look at whatever the user attached, linked, or pasted alongside the invocation — a URL, a screenshot, a block of text, a document excerpt. If nothing is attached and the subject isn't otherwise clear from the conversation, ask what to explain.

## 2. Ask three quick questions before writing

Check these before drafting, one at a time, and skip any the user has already answered in this conversation or made obvious in how they invoked the skill:

- **Who's this for** — you, personally, or someone else you'll relay it to? If someone else, get their name/role so the tone can match them.
- **How deep** — a pure ELI5 (assume zero prior knowledge, simplest possible framing) or calibrated to your actual familiarity with this specific topic (simpler for things you know nothing about, more substantive for things you're semi-familiar with)?
- **Analogy or not** — should the explanation lean on an analogy to make the concept click, or just explain it straight?

## 3. Research as needed

Work primarily from what's provided (the site, screenshot, snippet), but don't stop there if it references things that need context to make sense — an unfamiliar company, product, acronym, or concept. Look those up before writing so the explanation is accurate, not just plausible-sounding.

## 4. Write the explanation

- **Lead with a one-line TL;DR** — the single sentence that captures what this is / what it means, in plain language.
- Follow with a short explanation underneath, broken into clearly readable pieces — use headers and bullet points where they aid scanning. No walls of text.
- No jargon or internals unless requested — translate technical terms into what they mean in practice.
- If an analogy was requested, weave one in and carry it through consistently rather than mixing several.
- Match tone to the audience from step 2 (a touch more casual for personal use or a friend, more neutral for a colleague/manager).
- Keep it tight: TL;DR + a few short sections, not an essay.

## 5. Output

Write the explanation directly in your response as normal text, with headers/bullets as needed. No clipboard copying — the explanation itself is the deliverable.
